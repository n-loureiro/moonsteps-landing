Brief for the agent

This page is a self-contained problem formulation. The task is to turn it into a buildable design: a set-up that measures the total hemispherical, blackbody-weighted emissivity of spacecraft thermal-control materials from below 30 K to 370 K, in the sense of ECSS-Q-ST-70-09C. The method choice, the architecture and a first uncertainty budget are already made below. What remains is detailed design, CAD, analysis and documentation, held to the acceptance criteria in this section.

Deliver, as one package:

  1. A thermal model of the cryostat and sample stations that reproduces the signal levels in Table 3 and shows that the 2nd stage holds a stable set-point at the worst case of about 14 W (four ε = 0.95 stations at 370 K).
  2. The CAD package of Table 6, starting from the first-attempt schematics for a 50 × 50 × 5 mm sample: native files plus STEP AP242 with PMI, PDF drawings with GD&T, materials, finishes and coatings, and a BOM with part numbers and suppliers.
  3. A complete uncertainty budget workbook (GUM plus Monte Carlo) that extends Table 5 to every material class in Table 1, including foils.
  4. Electrical wiring, vacuum P&ID, interface control document (sample envelope, mounting and foil bonding procedure), software specification, calibration and validation plan.

Acceptance: every requirement row of Table 1 is traced to a design feature or an analysis; U (k = 2) < 0.04 is shown for each material class, or the class is explicitly restricted (for example thick insulators without a front thermometer); drawings carry every tolerance named on this page. Keep the facts and numbers here unless an analysis shows them wrong, and say so when it does.

Open decisions to put to the customer before detailed design: the 30 K versus 40 K floor open, whether thick non-metallic substrates are in scope open, and whether the optional room-temperature module M2 is wanted open.

Requirements

The set-up will be used to characterise materials used across spacecraft: radiators, telescope baffles, MLI and similar. The specification asks the supplier to pick the method (calorimetric, radiative or spectral), allows several modules for different samples or temperature ranges, and weighs performance, uncertainty, measurement time, installation, maintenance, handling and running cost.

Choice of method

The primary method is steady-state, guarded calorimetry in a cryocooler-cooled black enclosure. This is the hemispherical emittance approach of ECSS-Q-ST-70-09C, and of ASTM C835 extended to cryogenic temperatures. Spectral and radiative methods serve only as a room-temperature cross-check.11 ECSS-Q-ST-70-09C defines normal emittance by IR reflectometer and hemispherical emittance calorimetrically; the requirement here is the blackbody-weighted hemispherical value.

Spectral methods fail on two counts. At 30 K the energy sits in the far IR and sub-millimetre range (Table 2), where FTIR measurements have almost no signal and no integrating spheres or standards exist. And an FTIR gives directional reflectance, which then has to be integrated over angle to reach a hemispherical value; that integration adds its own uncertainty and is worst for specular mirrors. A calorimetric measurement delivers the total hemispherical, blackbody-weighted value directly, because the sample emits into a cold black enclosure. It treats diffuse, specular and multilayer samples alike.

The sample sits on a heated carrier inside a guard. Once temperatures are stable, the heater power P gives the apparent emissivity, which is then corrected for the shroud not being perfectly black:

ε_app = (P − P_par(T_s)) / (σ · A · (T_s⁴ − T_w⁴))

ε     = ε_app / (1 − ε_app · (A / A_w) · (1/ε_w − 1))

T_s, A     sample temperature and emitting area
T_w, A_w   shroud temperature and area,  ε_w  shroud emissivity
P_par      parasitic loss (wires, supports, gas), characterised in commissioning

Design consequences

Five decisions follow from the signal levels, and they drive everything else.

The shroud runs at 4–10 K for samples at 30–40 K. With a 77 K liquid-nitrogen shroud a 30 K sample would absorb heat rather than emit it, so liquid nitrogen is ruled out and a two-stage pulse-tube cooler is needed. At 4 K the back-radiation term (T_w/T_s)⁴ is about 3×10⁻⁴ at 30 K.

Set-points are approached from below and held by a controller. Left to itself, a low-ε sample at 30 K has a time constant C/G of about 10⁶ s. When the controller holds T_s constant, C·dT/dt is zero and P equals the loss directly. The sequence is therefore: cool everything, then step upward through the temperature ladder (Figure 2).

Back and edges are actively guarded. Only the front face may lose net heat. A 50×50×10 mm sample has 2000 mm² of edge against 2500 mm² of face, so the edges cannot be ignored. A guard cup is servoed to ΔT ≈ 0 against the carrier, and every lead is heat-sunk on the guard so it sees no gradient.

The shroud is black in the far IR. Ordinary black paints turn reflective above about 50 µm. The design combines a far-IR absorber (Stycast 2850FT loaded with SiC grit and carbon black) with a grooved or baffled geometry and an area ratio A/A_w < 0.01, so the cavity behaves as near-black even if the coating only reaches ε_w ≈ 0.5.

Several stations share one cooldown. Four sample stations, baffled from each other, amortise the 24–36 h cooldown.

The instrument is organised as modules. M1 is the cryo-calorimetric emissometer, the primary instrument for all sample types from 30 to 370 K, with a carrier family for 19, 25 and 50 mm samples, shims for 0.5–10 mm thickness and a foil carrier. M2 is optional: an FTIR with a gold integrating sphere (2–25 µm) or a portable IR reflectometer such as the AZ Tech TEMP 2000A, used to cross-check normal emittance at 300 K and to screen incoming samples.

Architecture

Hardware

Table 4 lists the subsystems. The common thread is that every watt crossing into the sample carrier is either measured (the heater) or nulled (the guard), and every surface the sample sees is cold and black.

Sample mounting

Rigid samples on metal substrates sit on a 0.1 mm indium foil, which works from cryogenic temperatures up to 370 K (indium melts at 430 K). Clamping is from the rear and sides, with pocket set-screws or a spring back-plate, and flushness is set with shims. Front masks are avoided; if one is unavoidable it belongs to the sample assembly and its emission is subtracted as a correction.

Foils and MLI are bonded to a 1 mm polished OFHC copper plate with a thin cryo-rated adhesive (Stycast 1266 or a low-outgassing acrylic transfer tape), laminated under vacuum or with a roller so there are no voids. At 370 K and ε = 0.95, a ~50 µm glue layer gives about 0.3 K across it, which is acceptable but goes in the budget. Metallised MLI foils go metallised side down. For semi-transparent foils the result depends on the backing: use gold-plated backing, which emulates VDA, and record it in the report.

Thick low-conductivity samples are the hard case. At 370 K and ε = 0.95, a 10 mm sample with k = 0.3 W/m·K has about 30 K between its back and its face. Mitigations, in order of preference: require metallic substrates for coatings; bond a 25 µm thermocouple to the front surface at a corner; or apply a conduction correction from a known k or from two thicknesses. This is the main material-dependent uncertainty term risk.

Uncertainty budget

Four cases bracket the material range (Table 5). Relative terms dominate for high-ε samples; absolute parasitic and guard terms dominate for low-ε samples and for samples at 30–40 K; the substrate gradient dominates for thick insulators. Report U per material class rather than as one number.

At 30 K the signal is about 0.5 µW, so the parasitic and self-heating term grows. The full 30 K target is feasible only with a 4 K shroud and good guarding. The 40–350 K fallback allowed by the specification is a budget relief, not a physical limit. Without a front-surface thermometer or a known conductivity, the instrument can claim U < 0.04 only for samples on metallic substrates.

Measurement cycle and running cost

A cycle runs pump-down (2 h), cooldown (24–36 h), a ladder of about ten points per station (½–3 h each, longest at low temperature and low ε) and warm-up (about 24 h). With four stations that is about four samples per 3–4 day cycle. Running cost is compressor electricity and cooling water plus cooler maintenance; there is no liquid helium or liquid nitrogen.

CAD package

3D models are delivered as native files plus STEP AP242 with PMI; drawings as PDF with GD&T, material, finish and coating callouts; the BOM in Excel or CSV with part numbers and suppliers.

Supporting analysis and records

  • Thermal model (ESATAN-TMS or COMSOL): stage loads per regime, cooldown time, parasitic conduction, shroud gradients, guard-mismatch sensitivity, sample gradient versus k.
  • Structural check of the cold-mass supports for handling and cooldown stresses; launch loads do not apply.
  • Interface control document: sample envelope, mounting rules, foil bonding procedure.
  • Calibration records: thermometers (ITS-90), SMU and DMM, heater R(T), area metrology, reference samples.
  • P_par characterisation: the two-area method and the gold-reference test.
  • Validation: inter-laboratory comparison report.
  • Software source, user manual, maintenance plan (cooler service intervals, spares).
  • The uncertainty budget workbook (GUM plus Monte Carlo).

Plan and risks

Four risks deserve early attention. The far-IR blackness of the shroud: coat and measure a coupon in the concept phase. The guard null at microwatt levels and 30 K. Cooler vibration coupling into the thermometry: use flexible braids and filtering. And thick insulating samples at high temperature, covered above.
